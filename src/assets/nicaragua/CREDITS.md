# Nicaragua water photography — credits

Openly licensed photographs of Nicaragua's water situation, collected for use on the
ImpactBridge site. Every file here is Creative Commons or public domain, sourced from
Wikimedia Commons. `credits.json` holds the same data in machine-readable form, and
`src/lib/photos.ts` exposes it to the app.

## Licence obligations

- **Attribution is required** for every CC image below. Show the photographer's name
  wherever the photo appears (a caption or a small credit line is enough), and link the
  licence.
- **CC BY-SA** additionally requires that if you *adapt the image itself* — crop
  heavily, composite, recolour — the adapted image is released under the same licence.
  Placing an unmodified photo in a page layout is not an adaptation.
- The U.S. Navy photograph is a public-domain work of the U.S. federal government and
  carries no attribution requirement, though the credit is kept for accuracy. Its
  presence is not a military or governmental endorsement of this project.

## Notes on provenance

Most of the set comes from a single 2011 photo essay by **Neil Palmer** for CIAT (the
International Center for Tropical Agriculture), documenting drought in Nicaragua and the
rainwater-harvesting reservoirs built in response. Because it is one coherent body of
work, the "before" and "after" images share a place and a visual language — useful when
pairing a problem image with the response to it.

Two caveats worth knowing before these go on a page:

- The CIAT photographs are **agricultural** water — reservoirs for dry-season irrigation
  — rather than household drinking water. They are honest illustrations of water scarcity
  in Nicaragua, but do not caption them as drinking-water supply projects.
- `managua-treatment-plant.jpg` is the author's own photo of a public facility; the
  Commons author field records only a forum profile URL, with the name given in the
  permission statement.

Source file pages are recorded per-image in `credits.json` if you need to re-verify a
licence.
